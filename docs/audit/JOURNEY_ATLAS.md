## Personas

### Novice — heard "Airship runs AI in your browser with no account", knows ChatGPT, does not know what a vault, attestation, posture or profile is. Wants to ask a question and get a real answer, then come back tomorrow.

> Airship's honesty machinery is genuinely excellent in prose but broken in state: on a cold first open the only warning-coloured line on screen falsely tells a brand-new user they already lost a conversation, and a single browser refresh silently destroys everything they typed while the recents list and the "All conversations" audit surface both report that nothing is missing.

#### First open — "what is this, and can I just ask something?"

- **Need.** They were told this runs AI in the browser with no account. They open the URL expecting a ChatGPT-like box. They need to learn, in about five seconds, what this is and what to do first.
- **Starting state.** Nothing configured. Empty localStorage except `airship.display-preferences.v1`; only IndexedDB is `airship-local-device-keyring-v1`. No credential, no conversation, no reload has occurred.
- **Shortest path.** Navigate to http://localhost:4173/?airshipLabNamespace=novice-first-d → app self-navigates `/` → `#chat` → `#chat/b5d597f8…` → `#chat/4ec62a4a…` and settles on an empty chat with three cards: "Open a terminal", "Browse the workspace", "Connect a model".
- **Advanced path.** The amber topbar CTA "Connect a model" (802,13, 131×32) is the highest-contrast control on screen and routes straight to `#connection`. The rail exposes Chat / Workspace / Memory / Proof and a GLOBAL group of Vault / Connection / Account. Command palette at ⌘. Nothing is hidden from a researcher.
- **In-flight states.** Observed the app write its own hash twice before settling: `#chat` → `#chat/b5d597f8-8b9b-43e8-9ce3-b0d011f59b2f` → `#chat/4ec62a4a-d7f1-4f79-8524-a31eb79a7b32`. A false loss notice paints at 249 ms (desktop) / 911 ms (mobile) and never clears — still present at 6 s.
- **Failure and recovery.** Clicked the topbar line "Local Device Vault needs a saved recovery key before first use". Nothing happened; URL stayed at `#chat/17729e23-466a-4d26-bfd0-d6d499d3795e`. Its DOM chain is SPAN.runtime-line__text → SPAN.runtime-line → DIV.topbar-actions → HEADER.topbar, `cursor: auto`, no button/anchor/role=button ancestor. The alarm offers no recovery from where it is raised.
- **Persistence.** Reproduced on 3/3 cold opens: desktop with namespace, desktop with no namespace in a fresh context, and mobile. The false notice survives to 6 s in all three.
- **Trust.** The first sentence Airship emphasises to a newcomer is a claim that their work was already destroyed. Everything else the product says about durability is calibrated and careful, so this single line teaches the user that Airship's warnings are noise — which is exactly the wrong lesson before they meet the real ephemerality in Journey 4.
- **Desktop vs mobile.** Desktop 1440×900: the notice renders in amber at y=880, the last and most saturated line on the page. Mobile 390×844: same notice, first painted at 911 ms. The vault warning measures 345×13 px on desktop but 0×0 px on mobile — present in the DOM and the accessibility tree, invisible to a sighted phone user.
- **Product change.** Route disclosure should be driven by the session the app actually holds, not by a hash the app wrote to itself. Canonicalising the URL for a newly created conversation must not re-enter the route-resolution path as a user-supplied address, and `UnknownSessionError` should only produce a loss notice when the requested id was one the user could plausibly have been given — i.e. when the request came from an inbound navigation, not from the app's own canonicalisation. Separately, any state line the product renders in its warning register must carry an action; "needs a saved recovery key" should be the same control that opens the Vault ceremony, and it should be laid out for the phone rather than clipped to zero.
- **Acceptance.** A test that opens a fresh context at `/` with empty storage, waits for the hash to settle, and asserts that no element matching /did not survive the reload/ ever appears — run at 1440×900 and 390×844. Plus: the vault status line resolves to a control whose click changes `location.hash` to `#vault`, and its bounding box has non-zero width at 390 px.

- **Advanced path.** Every honesty surface is reachable: the composer's credential-posture chip expands to "No provider credential is held. Replies come from a deterministic local demo and this conversation's journal is page memory only." - **Failure and recovery.** The reply does not pretend to answer: it says "Airship is running this turn entirely on your device with the deterministic demo provider. Retry and "Fork from here" are both offered, and "Edit & branch" carries an accurate explanation of what a branch reseeds.

#### Connect Chutes — the flow, as far as it goes with no credentials

- **Need.** They were promised "no account". They want the demo replaced by a real model with the least possible ceremony.
- **Starting state.** Demo session, no credential. Topbar shows the amber "Connect a model" CTA and the chip "No model connected · 5 ready".
- **Shortest path.** Click "Connect a model" → lands on `#connection` with the Chutes card already expanded at the top, other providers collapsed. Field "Chutes API key" with placeholder `cpk_…`, then "Discover models with key", then "Finish: verify & connect".
- **Advanced path.** The page descends properly: "Compare what each method can do" opens an eligibility matrix; OpenAI/Anthropic/xAI each carry a measured justification ("its token endpoint was measured returning access-control-allow-origin: *") behind "Why API key instead of OAuth?"; Ollama/LM Studio list the exact loopback allowlist `http://127.0.0.1:11434 · http://localhost:11434 · …`.

- **Failure and recovery.** With `cpk_notarealkey000000` the flow stops at Finish with an unusually good message: "Chutes did not accept this key. The catalog is readable without a key, so listing models succeeded; authorization is checked when you connect, and it failed. Check the key at chutes.ai → API keys ↗, or paste a different one." Network confirms it: `GET https://api.chutes.ai/e2e/instances/ac059e33-… auth=YES:Bearer cpk_notarealkey00` → `401`. Returning to `#chat` correctly shows the demo composer again, unchanged.
- **Persistence.** Nothing persists. On a new tab in the same namespace the key field is `""` and the chip is back to "No model connected · 5 ready". The card states this up front: "Held only in page memory."
- **Trust.** The stopping point is honest but the promise is not: a novice arrives believing no account is needed and discovers the only Chutes path is an account at chutes.ai plus a self-minted API key, phrased in build-engineer language. Then they learn the key must be re-pasted every session, which no one told them before they went to get it.
- **Desktop vs mobile.** Identical content and identical chip text on both. Mobile reaches Connection via the "More" sheet, which is well built — "Connection / Model providers and credentials", "Vault / Where your work is stored" — so navigation parity holds. The Chutes card is the first thing on the Connection page at both sizes.

Replace "not available in this build" with what the person can actually do and when. Most importantly, tell them the credential's lifetime before they leave to obtain one — the "page memory" fact belongs next to the "Create a key at chutes.ai" link as a consequence, with the durable alternative offered in the same breath. And count "ready" as connections that can serve a turn now, reporting the rest as what they are: available to set up.
- **Acceptance.** Pasting a well-formed but invalid key surfaces the authorization failure before any model, price or "direct session" chip renders, and no green success banner appears; assert the 401 to `/e2e/instances/` precedes the first render of the model picker. And the connection chip reads "0 ready" (or equivalent) while no provider can serve a turn.

#### Come back tomorrow — the conversation is gone and nothing says so

- **Need.** They asked something worth keeping and expect to find it later, the way every chat product they have used behaves.
- **Starting state.** One conversation with four messages, two receipts, title auto-set to "My dog is named Biscuit and my flight is Tuesday.", id `21feb15b-f93b-4374-9bea-4c51bb0aeab3`. Rail shows it with a real preview.
- **Shortest path.** Press reload. The app opens a brand-new conversation `2ac0bb75-6961-4d20-b99a-2d549c2f5c35`; the previous transcript is unrecoverable.
- **Advanced path.** The remedy does exist and is well built, but it is three levels deep: the unlabelled session chip → "Ephemeral · this page only / This session journal exists only in page memory. Nothing is synced. →" → `#vault`, which presents an excellent four-way comparison — "Survives closing the tab: Yes · encrypted on this device" vs "No · released with the page", "What can lose it: Browser eviction · clearing site data" vs "Closing the page".
- **In-flight states.** None. There is no warning before, during or after the loss — no "this will not be saved", no confirmation, no offer to keep it.
- **Failure and recovery.** Recovery is impossible and misdescribed. Navigating directly to the day-1 URL returns "That conversation existed only in page memory and did not survive the reload. This is a new conversation." — the same sentence the product wrongly shows on first open, which by now the user has been trained to ignore.
- **Persistence.** Measured: `AFTER RELOAD id: 2ac0bb75… sameAsDay1: false`, `AFTER RELOAD still has Biscuit: false`. The Chutes key is likewise gone. Only `airship.display-preferences.v1` survives.
- **Trust.** This is the whole promise failing at the last link, and it fails quietly. The conversation count stays at "1" across the loss, so the shelf looks undisturbed; the user is more likely to conclude they never sent the message than that Airship discarded it.
- **Desktop vs mobile.** Identical loss on both. The rail-based recents on desktop and the "All conversations / Search and resume past chats" entry in the mobile More sheet both show the same phantom entry, and both promise resumability that does not exist.
- **Product change.** Ephemerality is the single most consequential fact about a new user's first conversation and it is currently the least visible. It should be stated on the chat surface itself, in the empty state next to "The edge runtime is ready in this tab, with no account", with the Vault ceremony offered inline as the one-click remedy — the Vault comparison table is already good enough to carry that decision, it is simply unreachable. Until a durable store is adopted, the recents list must not present a fresh conversation in the slot of a destroyed one: a discarded conversation should either be absent with an explicit "1 conversation was not saved" line, or listed as discarded with the reason. "Ready to resume" and "Structure passed" must not be printed about a record with 0 messages that replaced a record with 4.
- **Acceptance.** Send two messages, reload, and assert (a) the recents list does not show an entry whose timestamp matches the destroyed conversation while reporting "No messages yet", and (b) either the destroyed conversation is reported as lost in visible text or the count decreases. Separately, assert the chat empty state on a cold open contains visible text stating conversations are not saved yet, and that it contains a control that reaches `#vault`.

### power-user — weeks in Airship, keyboard-first, three profiles (General/Research/Developer), many conversations, wants speed and wants to branch a thread without losing the thread

> Airship's expert layer exists and works — eight `g` chords, ⌘K, ⌘\, per-profile drafts, real profile silos — but it is taught nowhere except inside the palette it can't teach you to open, and the one power move that matters most (Edit & branch) announces "Carrying 4 ancestor messages" onto a screen with zero messages on it.

#### Reach any surface without leaving the home row

- **Need.** After weeks of use the rail is muscle memory and too slow. I want ⌘K and single-key chords to move between chat, All conversations, workspace, terminal, memory and proof, and I want to know which chords exist without reading source.
- **Starting state.** Three profiles configured, several conversations in the active profile, chat open at #chat/<id>. The topbar shows a ⌘ glyph button, so I assume a palette exists. I have never been shown a shortcut list.
- **Shortest path.** Click once in the transcript (to leave the composer) → press `g` then `s` → lands on #sessions. Or ⌘K → type `all conv` → ↵. - **Failure and recovery.** Pressed `?`, `F1` and `Shift+/` on the chat route: no dialog opened in any case (measured `[...document.querySelectorAll('[role=dialog]')]` → `[]` each time). Nothing about the keyboard layer is listed, configurable, or discoverable after reload — the chord set is only ever visible as trailing text inside palette row descriptions ("Chat · Work · Session scope · g c").
- **Trust.** The product's fastest layer is invisible: a user who never opens the palette will never learn that eight chords exist, and a user who does open it learns them as a side-effect of reading descriptions. That reads as an unfinished expert mode rather than a deliberate one.
- **Desktop vs mobile.** At 390x844 the topbar palette button is absent (`.topbar-actions > .icon-button { display: none; }`, routes.css:2563) — but parity holds through More → "Command Center · Search every route, recent session, and slas…", measured 3 taps / 3365ms to jump into a named conversation. Where a surface owns the keyboard (terminal, editor), it should state its own escape chord in its status line.

#### Switch profiles mid-thought without losing the thread

- **Need.** I keep General for everyday work, Research for sourcing and Developer for builds. I switch several times an hour and need to know exactly what came with me and what stayed behind — draft, terminal, workspace, conversation.
- **Starting state.** General active, an unfinished draft in the composer, a live WebContainer terminal in /workspace with a file I created, and the workspace tree open.
- **Shortest path.** Click the "Agent profile" control in the topbar (or the rail monogram) → pick "RE Research" from a 3-row listbox ("GE General ✓ / RE Research / DE Developer") → the whole shell re-themes and a new Research conversation opens.
- **Advanced path.** `g p` reaches #profiles, which is the manifest editor ("IMMUTABLE AGENT MANIFESTS … Saves create content-addressed revisions; switching restores that profile's most recent compatible conversation") with a per-profile "Switch to this profile" button — a slower, more powerful route that also exposes workspace binding, memory scope, approval policy and minimum proof posture.
- **In-flight states.** Sampling the topbar every 50ms after the click: "Research cockpit started" present at t=51ms, 103ms and 154ms, absent by t=205ms, replaced by the standing warning "Local Device Vault needs a saved recovery key before first use". The new conversation carries a durable transcript line that survives the toast: "Research had no compatible conversation, so Airship started one."
- **Failure and recovery.** Switching back to General restored its conversation with the marker "Selected as this profile's active conversation. Event 2 · profile.active-conversation.selected · sha256:20Z-BGyi…" and the composer draft came back byte-for-byte: "DRAFT-GENERAL: unfinished question about the audit trail". Research's composer was empty (""), which is the right answer — drafts are per-profile.
- **Persistence.** Silos hold. ### keyboard-a11y — a keyboard-only screen-reader user, mouse never touched, driving Airship at 1440x900 and 390x844 with Tab / Shift+Tab / arrows / Enter / Escape only, and reading state through accessible names and live-region text changes rather than pixels.

## P0 root cause — the service worker reloads the page mid-journey

Driven directly against the production build with a fresh browser context, which
is what a first visit actually is.

**Mechanism.** The service worker installs, takes control, and `controllerchange`
fires. `platform-shell.tsx` reloads the document when that happens and the person
has not yet interacted — deliberately, so COOP/COEP are established before anyone
starts working. On a fresh context that takeover lands *during* the Vault → chat
transition rather than at first paint.

**Measured**, one run, fresh context, Local Device Vault preselected:

| step | main-frame navigations | notes |
|---|---|---|
| after the Vault ceremony reports Ready | 1 | |
| after `goto #chat` and the vault-active chip appears | **3** | two extra: the document reloaded |
| at that moment | — | `.pwa-update` banner present, `role="status"` |

**What it costs a person.** The conversation that existed before the reload was
page-memory, so it does not survive it. Everything downstream follows from that
one fact:

- the first screen reporting "That conversation existed only in page memory and
  did not survive the reload" to someone who had done nothing (fixed separately
  by scoping the notice — the reload itself was not addressed);
- a turn sent in that window rendered, reported complete, and lost;
- the address afterwards resolving to a journal that cannot hold it, so the
  composer says "This conversation link is not available in the current runtime:
  Fork required" beside a topbar reading "Encrypted Local Device vault active";
- the update banner intercepting pointer events over the composer — measured as
  `<div role="status" class="pwa-update"> intercepts pointer events` while
  clicking Send.

**Why it hid.** A warm service worker has no update pending, so a second run in
the same profile passes. Ten consecutive runs outside Playwright survived 10/10
for exactly this reason. The gating harness uses a fresh context every time,
which is the honest case and the one that fails.

**The repair.** Two separable parts:

1. The takeover must not discard work. Either hold the reload until the page has
   nothing unsaved (the existing `userInteracted` fence is too narrow — a
   conversation exists before anyone types), or commit the page-memory session to
   the adopted authority before reloading.
2. A `role="status"` banner may not cover the composer. It is an announcement,
   and it is currently a pointer-event surface over the primary control.
